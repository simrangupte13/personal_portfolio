Partnership Strategy &
Business Communications

Client Relations Campaign Strategy Negotiation Stakeholder Management

I joined Restaurant & Bar Design Awards in 2021 as a design researcher. A few months in, my role grew to cover project coordination, communications, content and partnership strategy, and I began working directly with the Founder.

The Awards partnered with hospitality suppliers, designers and operator groups around the world, offering partners an extensively followed platform to build brand visibility and create meaningful business connections.

Partnerships took different forms: paid sponsorships, service-for-promotion exchanges, and venue partnerships with hospitality groups in exchange for digital visibility.

Challenge

Our outreach worked, but what partners wanted was changing. They asked for flexibility and relevance instead of a fixed list of sponsorship benefits.

The challenge was to stop selling set packages and build each partnership around what the brand/company needed. That meant balancing sponsorship value, promotional value and the partner’s internal budget.

A successful partnership didn’t always mean a closed deal. Keeping a strong relationship with a potential partner, even when timing or budget ruled out a collaboration, was part of the process.

Approach

I shifted the conversation from “here's what we can offer” to “what are you trying to achieve, and who are you trying to reach?”

Every conversation began with discovery. I learned the partner’s objectives, audience and priorities before proposing anything. Outreach started with a short, personalised email, followed by a call to explore the opportunity. Marketing proposals and campaign strategies came next, built around the partner’s goals instead of a predetermined package/list.

This made partnership development consultative. I built the connection first and shaped the collaboration around it.

Outcome

Over 3.5 years, I helped close 30+ partnerships, with several partners renewing across multiple years.

Partners consistently pointed to two benefits: direct access to a highly targeted industry audience, and greater visibility and brand recognition from campaigns built around their objectives.

Partnership development stopped being a transactional sponsorship conversation. It became a way to understand a business problem, agree upon a shared objective and build a communications solution around it.

TYPE

Campaign Strategy Digital Project Management Social Media Stakeholder Alignment

TYPE was one of the engagement campaigns I managed during Restaurant & Bar Design Awards’ year-long branding partnership with its 2026 Creative Partner. It ran for eight weeks, between the shortlist and winners announcements.

Historically, this period saw a drop in social engagement. The objective was twofold: re-engage the Awards' Instagram audience while creating meaningful visibility and value for the Creative Partner.

Challenge

In a digital-only awards format, a new Creative Partner has no physical moment to meet the industry it is now associated with.

Visibility was only part of what the partner wanted. They also wanted direct access, genuine introductions and the chance to connect with the designers and operators they’d want to work with.

The challenge was to create those one-to-one connections and keep the audience engaged within a fixed eight-week window. The target was roughly 600K profile views in 30 days, against 350K in 2025.

Approach

The campaign was keyword-led and built on one idea: every space starts as an idea before it becomes physical.

Each shortlisted project was invited to reply with one word that captured the essence of its design. The Creative Partner then handpicked the projects they wanted to connect with. They responded personally to each one and made a custom visual from the project’s keyword and image. This put the partner in direct conversation with some of the strongest names in hospitality design and turned passive visibility into a genuine introduction.

Taking part was deliberately easy. Shortlisted projects received one bulk email with a template, so they could respond in seconds without creating new content or writing a long submission.

Behind the scenes, I coordinated the partner workflow through a shared tracker. It followed each asset from design through scheduling and publishing and kept the eight-week timeline on track.

I also wrote the social media copy for each feature and developed a monthly newsletter around the campaign, which achieved a 42% open rate.

Outcome

62% of shortlisted projects participated, exceeding the platform's 50% engagement benchmark. The campaign reached 740K+ profile views on Instagram in 30 days, against 600K target, and 350K the previous year.

It created direct, personal introductions between the Creative Partner and leading hospitality design projects. Shortlisted participants gained extra recognition and value beyond their original entry.

For the platform, it sustained audience engagement through what has historically been its quietest period.

The campaign served three goals through one mechanism: collaboration.

2025 Awards Ceremony

Project Management Creative Operations Stakeholder Management Cross-functional Coordination Event Design

The 2025 Restaurant & Bar Design Awards ceremony took place in Dubai. It brought together an international network of 400+ hospitality designers and operators, creative partners, sponsors and suppliers for an immersive event.

I coordinated the creative production of the ceremony from ideation to execution over 12 months. I turned the creative vision into a structured programme of deliverables, timelines and dependencies, and kept multiple workstreams and cross-functional teams moving towards the same deadline.

The project ran from early teaser communications to the live ceremony. It covered event branding, digital campaigns, social content, website assets, video, animation, and on-site digital and wayfinding graphics. The stakeholders included the identity partner, creative partners, sound partner, venue and audiovisual teams, social media, event speakers, the costume designer, media and more.

BRIEF & CHALLENGES

The brief covered far more than designing an event.

The ImaginAIre concept had to appear across the whole audience journey: the first announcement and website experience, social media, newsletters, invitations, ceremony screens, physical branding and post-event content.

The project involved external partners Glasfurd & Walker, 4SPACE Design, SPECTRE and Effe Hospitality, as well as the five journey designers, sponsors, venue partners and internal teams.

The volume of deliverables was only part of the challenge. The rest was coordinating multiple creative disciplines and stakeholders around one idea, across different timelines, dependencies, formats and stages of production.

Digital

  • Website landing page and video
  • Instagram and LinkedIn campaigns
  • Newsletter and email marketing assets
  • Event invitations and ticketing assets
  • Teaser and announcement content

Ceremony

  • Presentation templates across all categories
  • Event sound and lighting design
  • Narrator slides

Post-event

  • Photography and video handovers

PROJECT MANAGEMENT APPROACH

The project ran from September 2024 through the ceremony and into November 2025. My first priority was to make the whole production lifecycle transparent.

I built and maintained a master timeline covering creative development, stakeholder input, asset handovers, communications milestones and event deadlines. It let different workstreams progress in parallel and made their dependencies visible.

I used Asana, Google Workspace, Later and Figma to manage planning, collaboration, content workflows and creative review.

The approach centred on four areas:

01. Planning
Breaking the creative brief into defined workflows, deliverables and milestones.

02. Coordination
Keeping internal teams, creative partners, suppliers and event stakeholders aligned on what was needed, by whom and by when.

03. Dependencies
Identifying where one deliverable or team relied on another, and sequencing the work to match.

04. Delivery
Tracking progress through reviews, handovers and final deadlines, so assets were ready for their intended channel or event.

Managing dependencies

A key part of the process was sequencing handovers so one team’s output became another team’s starting point. For example:

Designer journeys → creative write up and images from Stakeholder A, B, C, D & E → AI videos to be developed by Partner A → sound design by Partner B → final video review by internal team → testing at venue → ceremony presentation

while simultaneously:

Creative concept → identity assets designed by Partner C → sound design by Partner B → social / newsletter content management by internal team → ceremony communications

The project timeline mapped these dependencies alongside the wider communications and event schedule, so multiple workflows could move forward with the event deadline in view. This mattered most as the project moved from concept development into campaign production and then live-event production, when the number of interdependent deliverables rose sharply.

THE EVENT

The ceremony took place at EPIK Dubai on 27 October 2025, after months of creative development, production, reviews and handovers.

My role was to bring the parallel workflows together into one coordinated event. The visual identity, designer journeys, AI films, sound, presentation system, physical branding and digital communications all had to work as a single experience.

The creative concept carried consistently across the screen, the space and the audience journey, supported by the production structure and coordination behind it.

Partnership & Business Development

{{ writingArrow0 }}

Turning a partner's objectives into a relevant proposition.

CONTEXT

A proposal built around the client's stated objectives: global visibility, thought leadership and meaningful industry connections. Rather than presenting a single sponsorship package, I developed three routes with different levels of engagement and value.

Writing Sample

Hi [Name],

I hope you're well.

Apologies for keeping you waiting.

Following our conversation, we discussed your objectives internally and put together a few options that we believe align well with your goals of increasing global brand visibility and building meaningful connections within the hospitality design industry.

Option 1: Awards Partnership - [Commercial Proposal]
We would love to welcome [Hospitality Brand] as a Partner of the upcoming [Awards Program]. As an Awards Partner, you would receive:
- Global Brand Visibility
Prominent brand placement across the Awards website and newsletters throughout the Awards cycle, reaching a global audience of hospitality and design professionals.
- Thought-Leadership Feature
An interview with your team, creating a dedicated feature around [relevant design category/topic], promoted across our editorial and social channels.
- Personal Introductions
A senior member of our team would personally introduce you to a curated selection of influential hospitality professionals and decision-makers. The list would be developed collaboratively with your team.
- Exclusive Branding Rights
Permission to use the [Awards Programme] partnership identity across your website and marketing materials.

Option 2: Brand Spotlight - [Commercial Proposal]
This option provides year-round visibility through:
- Global Brand Visibility
Prominent brand placement across the Awards website and newsletters throughout the Awards cycle.
- Thought-Leadership Feature
An interview with your team, creating a dedicated feature around [relevant design category/topic], promoted across our editorial and social channels.

Option 3: Exclusive Thought-Leadership Feature - [Commercial Proposal]
We discussed the proposed [specific category] internally and, unfortunately, don't believe it would be the strongest fit for our audience. However, we do see a strong opportunity to position [Hospitality Brand] as a thought leader within [relevant category/topic].

We would interview your team and create a dedicated feature exploring trends, innovation and the future of the category, promoted across our newsletter and social channels.

This would allow your team to share its expertise with a global audience while gaining exposure among designers, operators and hospitality professionals.

Please let us know your thoughts on the above options. We'd be happy to discuss them further and explore how we can tailor a collaboration to best support your objectives.

Best,
Simran Gupte

Managing Creative Differences

{{ writingArrow2 }}

Resolving creative differences between cross-functional teams to avoid conflict.

CONTEXT

When two creative teams were developing different parts of the same identity, the communication needed to protect both sides' work while finding a way to create cohesion. The message acknowledged both perspectives, proposed a practical route forward and created a space for the teams to resolve it together.

Writing Sample

Hi [Name],

Just to share an update - we met with [Brand Name] yesterday, where they presented their concept for the trophy box design. Here's the presentation for your reference: [Link]

Following the discussion, and to make sure neither of your creative efforts or time is wasted, we talked through a possible way to bring everything together into one cohesive identity i.e. there needs to be a very strong visual connection.

The idea would be for [Brand Name] to share the colour codes for the identity colour palette so [Brand Name] can align with this. Also, [Brand Name] could, in very small doses, begin incorporating some of the patterns [Brand Name] has been developing, without changing the overall theme. One example discussed was integrating these patterns into the non-header webpage videos within your existing video collage.

We definitely don’t want to disrupt your flow of work leading up to the identity reveal, so any of the above can be introduced later and at your own pace.

I think it would be helpful to have a quick meeting with everyone for more clarity, and it would also be great for both teams to meet directly. How about [Day] [Date] at [Time]?

If that works, I’ll share a calendar invite.

Best,
Simran Gupte

De-escalating Negative Feedback

{{ writingArrow1 }}

Protecting the relationship when the answer isn't what someone wanted to hear.

CONTEXT

When a partner was disappointed by a change to the Awards format, the response needed to acknowledge their frustration without becoming defensive. I focused on what could still be offered that they would benefit from even more than they would have before.

Writing Sample

Hi [Name], Thank you for being honest with us. Please know that we truly appreciate all the work that you have already put into this, and we made you aware as soon as we made the decision. [Name] is more than happy to personally introduce you to anybody and everybody you wish. We have an expansive network of [People]. This can be done via email, in a format which you're happy with. Just give us a shout, and [Name] will sort these out for you. Additionally, [Name] must have already gotten in touch with you regarding the [Program], which is a lifetime listing that you will have with us. We do understand that you were looking forward to the ceremony, but we hope that you do see how this update will shift focus onto digital media where you'll be able to choose how to market [Brand Name]. There's plenty of opportunities and we're very open to your creative ideas. Best, Simran Gupte

Making a cold introduction relevant, and outlining clear next steps.

[Event Series] is an exclusive, invitation-only event that brings together a curated group of the most influential designers and hospitality operators in the world to explore the relationship between design, light, and emotion. Our most recent edition was hosted at [Venue], in partnership with [Brand Name]. I would love to share more details. Would you be open to a conversation? Best, Simran Gupte
